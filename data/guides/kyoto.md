# Kyoto

Kyoto is Japan's old imperial capital and still its cultural heart, with more
than a thousand temples and shrines tucked between the mountains. Spring brings
cherry blossom and autumn brings fiery maple leaves; both are spectacular and
both are crowded, so a visit in early summer or late autumn trades peak colour
for breathing room.

Getting around leans on buses more than trains — Kyoto's subway is small, just
two lines, so a day bus pass is often the better buy. Rent a bicycle if the
weather is kind; the city is flat and riverside paths link many of the sights.

The temples reward an early start: Fushimi Inari's endless vermilion torii gates
are magical before the tour groups arrive, and the Arashiyama bamboo grove is
quietest at dawn. Save an evening for Gion, the geisha district, where lantern-lit
wooden machiya houses line the lanes and a careful stroll feels like stepping
back a century.
